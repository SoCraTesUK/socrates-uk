## Make the case

Thinking about coming to SoCraTes UK but need to square it with work first? This page
is here to help. Below you will find the case for attending, an honest summary of the
costs, and an email you are welcome to adapt and send to your manager. There is also a
note on bringing a colleague along, because SoCraTes is better shared.

<br>

### Why it is worth your time

SoCraTes UK is not a conventional conference. There is no fixed programme and no line-up
of vendors selling to you. It is an [Open Space unconference][open_space_unconference]:
the attendees build the agenda together each morning, and the sessions are proposed and
run by the people in the room. That format is exactly why it earns its place in a busy
year.

1. **You set the agenda.** Bring the real problems your team is wrestling with and put
   them to a room full of experienced practitioners. You are not limited to whatever a
   programme committee chose months ago — if it matters to you, you can propose a session
   on it and have the conversation you actually need.

2. **You learn from people doing the work.** The room is full of developers, testers and
   technical leaders from a wide range of organisations. The learning is practical and
   candid: what worked, what did not, and what they would do differently. Much of the
   value comes from the honesty you rarely get in a polished conference talk.

3. **The learning comes back with you.** Attendees are encouraged to take notes, share
   what they picked up, and run a session or write it up for their own team afterwards.
   One person going can lift the whole team, which makes it straightforward to justify.

4. **You come back connected.** Two full days together, plus the evenings, build the kind
   of relationships that keep paying off long after the event — people to ask when you are
   stuck, and a wider community to draw on. For hiring and for reputation, being part of
   that community is worth a great deal.

5. **It is genuinely hands-on.** Expect pairing, mob programming, whiteboards and working
   through problems together, not a day of sitting and listening. People come away with
   techniques and ideas they can put to use the following Monday.

6. **It is run at cost.** SoCraTes UK is organised by volunteers and makes no profit from
   tickets. Where sponsorship allows, the price is reduced and the difference is refunded
   to every attendee. You are paying for the venue, the rooms and the food — not a margin.

### What it costs

An honest summary, so there are no surprises when you ask. Prices are for SoCraTes UK
{{ site.event_details.year }}; see the [tickets page][tickets_page] for the full detail.

| Item | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| Open Space ticket (2 days, incl. private room, all meals and refreshments) | £675 |
| Optional [training day][training_day_page] the day before | £375 |
| Optional Wednesday-evening arrival (room, dinner and breakfast) | £110 |
| Travel to Milton Hill House, Abingdon, Oxfordshire | Varies |

The ticket price is all-in for the event itself: your room, breakfast, lunch and dinner,
and tea, coffee and biscuits throughout are all included. Because sponsorship is applied
equally across every ticket, the final cost is often lower than the headline — in 2024,
for example, attendees received a £125 rebate.

### An email you can send

Feel free to copy, cut down and make this your own.

> **Subject:** Attending SoCraTes UK {{ site.event_details.year }}
>
> Hi [manager],
>
> I would like to attend SoCraTes UK {{ site.event_details.year }}, a two-day software
> craft and testing unconference at Milton Hill House in Oxfordshire.
>
> It is not a typical conference. Rather than a fixed programme, the attendees set the
> agenda each morning and run the sessions themselves, so I can bring the specific
> problems we are working on and get practical input from experienced developers and
> testers from other organisations.
>
> The ticket is £675, which includes accommodation and all meals. It is run by
> volunteers at cost, so there is no profit built in, and the price is often reduced by
> sponsorship. I would expect to come back with concrete techniques I can share with the
> team, and I am happy to write up what I learn or run an internal session afterwards.
>
> Could we cover the ticket and travel? I think it is good value for what we would get
> out of it.
>
> Thanks,
> [your name]

### Bring a colleague

SoCraTes is better with company, and coming as a pair or a small group means you can
split up to cover more sessions and compare notes in the evening. It also makes the case
to your manager stronger: more of the team learning, for a shared trip.

If you would like to bring a partner, friend or colleague who is not attending the Open
Space, there is a `+1` option covering their room and meals. Colleagues who want to join
the Open Space itself simply need their own ticket. The [tickets page][tickets_page] has
the various combinations, and if you are not sure what fits, just [get in touch][contact].

And if you are worried about not knowing anyone — please do not be. The community works
hard to be welcoming, and we hope to pair newcomers with a
[Conference Buddy][conference_buddies] so no one arrives on their own.
